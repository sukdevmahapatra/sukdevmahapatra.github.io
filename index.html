About

Hello! I am Sukdev Mahapatra, an early-career researcher with an M.Sc. in Physics (Astroparticle Physics). I am passionate about understanding the fundamental nature of the Universe through theoretical and computational physics. My work involved modeling dark matter interactions with solar nuclei, calculating capture rates, and studying their implications for indirect dark matter detection through high-energy neutrinos.

My research interests include Dark Matter Phenomenology, Astroparticle Physics, Cosmology, and Particle Physics. I enjoy combining analytical techniques with computational methods to investigate fundamental questions in modern physics. I am currently preparing for doctoral research and actively seeking opportunities to contribute to cutting-edge research in dark matter, cosmology, and high-energy astrophysics.

Research

During my Master's research at the Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad (IIT Hyderabad), I investigated the capture, propagation, and thermalization of dark matter inside the Sun within the framework of Non-Relativistic Effective Field Theory (NR-EFT). My work focused on evaluating dark matter capture rates, thermalization timescales, and the resulting high-energy neutrino signals arising from long-lived mediators.

I am currently working on the research project "Non-equilibrium Thermalization of Heavy Dark Matter in the Sun and Neutrino Signals from Long-Lived Mediators" at IIT Hyderabad. This research investigates the non-equilibrium evolution of heavy dark matter captured by the Sun and its implications for indirect dark matter detection through neutrino observations.

In addition, I am also working on a project at IIT Goa on the "Inelastic Scattering of Solar Reflected Dark Matter". This work explores the phenomenology of solar reflected dark matter undergoing inelastic interactions, with the aim of understanding its detection prospects and its implications for future dark matter experiments.
